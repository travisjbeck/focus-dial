## The Story

I noticed that I am most productive when I set a timer to complete a task. This became even more apparent as I was learning to type again as there were drastically diminishing returns on learning after 25 minutes or so. 

So, I started looking for a "nice" timer to use. The nicer the better. I love beautiful objects and it seems like a super purposeful item like this is ripe for badass design and function. My initial search on amazon surfaced some decent cheap basic cheap options, and some very odd dated looking "pomodoro" specific timers. Nothing like I was looking for. I was looking for the "Peak Design" version of a workspace timer. Then, I found an indie gogo for exactly what I was looking for.   It had been funded in 48 hours and featured in Time Magazine, had tons of press, but sadly, they never shipped. Reading the company posts on indiegogo over time it became apparent these guys had a good idea, but no idea what they were doing. Even the product, upon closer inspection had some major flaws.  

Searching on, I came across a youtuber that was an original backer of the "Chi Timer" and wanted to see if he could just recreate it with readily available components and a 3D printer. He built something better in some ways, but lacking in others. He put the whole project on GitHub and created an instructable to walk people through building their own "Focus Timer". That video has been out 4 weeks and has 200,000 views. 


### The Spark

  It seems like there are a whole bunch of folks like me willing to shell out top dollar or spend many hours tinkering in order to have this product on their desks.  I get it.Its special. Its a beautiflu obnect that turns a mundane task into a ritual. rituals are powerful habit builders. People understand this. The best part is the mac and ios "Focus Mode" integrations as well as the unlimited integration potential of a custom webhook. 

  Let me see if I can build this and understand how it works. I want one, first of all, second of all, with some iteration this could be a commercially viable gadget that lots of people have already validated. I know this world. I know lots of people to make this happen. I've done it before and made a shit ton of invaluable mistakes that I don't have to make again. The intersection of software and hardware and design is the strongest gravitational pull I've ever felt in an idea. The challenge is real, and that's exciting.

Can we make simple electronics in a plastic / wood / metal shell in America? of course. we can just do things. Ahh, but how much will it cost?  As much as it costs.
